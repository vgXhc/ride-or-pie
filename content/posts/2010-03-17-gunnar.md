Finally the weather has been nice enough for me to take out Gunnar for a couple of rides. After a short loop through the city and a ride to the FLCC spring seminar two Sundays ago, yesterday I took Gunnar on a real test ride, out to Taughannock and back on Route 89. And Gunnar rides like a charm. I pushed it pretty hard and averaged at 30.7 km/h for the out-of-town portion of the ride. I think the geometry works pretty well for me, but I will keep tinkering with it: saddle height is maybe still a bit too low, but the current seat post is already at its max. And if I have to get a new seat post anyway, I might get one with a different set-back. Currently my KOPS (knee over pedal spindle) position is about 6 cm behind the spindle and my saddle is as far forward as possible. I'm well aware that <a href="http://www.sheldonbrown.com/kops.html">several</a> <a href="http://bikesnobnyc.blogspot.com/2008/12/fitment-issues-kops-vs-cops.html">cycling gurus</a> are rather skeptical of the KOPS method and their arguments are making a lot of sense to me. But given that my saddle is at the margin of adjustability, a zero-setback post would give me more options to tinker with my position. Probably I should just ask Glenn for advice. Handlebar height might get readjusted downwards, too. At the moment my posture is fairly aggressive, but I think I could drop a spacer or two and still be comfortable. <br />
<br />
I'll go for another ride with another German guy today and we'll see how Gunnar feels after 2 or 2.5 hours. I definitely expect my behind to hurt, as the <a href="http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/B001CSZXA6?ie=UTF8&tag=riorpi-20&linkCode=as2&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=B001CSZXA6">Selle Italia Flite Gel Flow</a><img src="http://www.assoc-amazon.com/e/ir?t=riorpi-20&l=as2&o=1&a=B001CSZXA6" width="1" height="1" border="0" alt="" style="border:none !important; margin:0px !important;" /> (I have the one without titanium rails) saddle is just too tiny. My knees might also be a trouble spot, as I messed them up a bit yesterday, courtesy of a downward sliding seat post...<br />
