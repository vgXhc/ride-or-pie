Ben and I have been on the road again. This is our third trip together: In 2024 we rode through the [Swiss and Italian Alps](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLVYvBhWNmbN1s1mKJQtem4c2NcO8DjMCT). And earlier this year, we did [a quick tour through Wisconsin](https://twobikersabroad.orangebikelabs.com/tag/spring2026/). And now we were in France, doing a weeklong loop from Geneva. On his blog, Ben has [day-by-day accounts of our trip](https://twobikersabroad.orangebikelabs.com/tag/alps2026/). In this post, I'll focus on the photos that I took along the way. 

I had gone back and forth on whether I should bring a "proper" camera. In the end, all these photos were taken with my Google Pixel 10, shot in RAW and edited in Lightroom. Because there a lot of photos, I'll split the post into two parts.

![Ben is riding a loaded gravel bike up a mountain. There are low hanging clouds shrouding the top of the mountains and the horizon, and the road is wet. The grass is mostly yellow.](/images/2026-alps/20260909-Ben-is-riding-a-loaded-gravel-bike-up-a-mountain.-There-are-low-hanging-clouds-shrouding-the-top-of-the-mountains-and-the-horizon,-and-the-road-is-wet.-The-grass-is-mostly-yellow-.jpg)

We got a late afternoon start from Geneva and rode up the Saléve. This photo is after the high point and on the way to the Col des Pitons. It had rained early in the day and the top of the mountain was still shrouded on low-hanging clouds.

![The top of Col des Pitons, with a sign marking the pass. There are trees lining the narrow road. The road is wet and the sky is very cloudy.](/images/2026-alps/20260909-The-top-of-Col-des-Pitons,-with-a-sign-marking-the-pass.-There-are-trees-lining-the-narrow-road.-The-road-is-wet-and-the-sky-is-very-cloudy-.jpg)

The Col des Pitons was the highest point of the day, and there it really was raining. We suited up accordingly for the descent toward Annecy, where we spent the night. 

![Pont Perrière in Annecy, over the Thiou River, with the morning sun in the background. Our two bikes are leaning against the railing. The railings along the river are decorated with flower pots.](/images/2026-alps/20260910-Pont-Perrière-in-Annecy,-over-the-Thiou-River,-with-the-morning-sun-in-the-background.-Our-two-bikes-are-leaning-against-the-railing.-The-railings-along-the-river-are-decorated-with-flower-pots-.jpg)

The hotel options in Annecy were limited by the time we booked, and so we ended up getting a fairly expensive place right in the old town. Because we got in so late, we couldn't make the best use of it in the evening, but in the morning it was nice to start from such a picturesque location.

![](/images/2026-alps/20260910-A-power-line-going-up-a-mountain-side.-The-power-tower-and-the-wires-partially-disappear-into-clouds.-The-ground-is-covered-with-brush-and-flowers-and-framed-by-forest-on-both-sides-.jpg)

We started climbing from Annecy immediately, toward the Semnoz/Crêt de Chatillon -- no warmup. It was sunny in the valley but the clouds were still hanging low over the mountain. It's a long and strenuous climb, gaining well over 1000 meters of elevation. 

![A power line going up a mountain side. The power tower and the wires partially disappear into clouds. The ground is covered with brush and flowers and framed by forest on both sides.](/images/2026-alps/20260910-The-Col-de-Plainpalais-pass-sign-and-Ben-approaching-the-pass-on-the-road.-The-sky-is-still-overcast-.jpg)

The descent from the Crêt de Chatillon was cold! We should have put on more layers. By the time we were in the valley, both of us were literally shivering. We stopped at a roadside stand for fresh fries, which slowly brought some warmth back. The second climb of the day was the Col de Plainpalais. This one was much easier and on very scenic and quiet back roads. And the descent into Chambery, where we spent the night, was a lot warmer.

![A winding mountain road. On the left of the road is a steep rock face with safety netting over it. In the background a power line.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-A-winding-mountain-road.-On-the-left-of-the-road-is-a-steep-rock-face-with-safety-netting-over-it.-In-the-background-a-power-line-.jpg)



Chambery was a fun city to stay in. I had a bit of time to walk around in the evening. The streets were bustling with activity. The next morning, we started the day climbing Col du Granier. It was a good pass to ride. Not too step, a cool tunnel, and great views of Mont Granier and its surroundings. 

![Selfie on top of the Col du Granier, with the pass sign and Mont Granier in the background](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Selfie-on-top-of-the-Col-du-Granier,-with-the-pass-sign-and-Mont-Granier-in-the-background.jpg)

![Mont Granier with the bright sun behind it.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Mont-Granier-with-the-bright-sun-behind-it-.jpg)

We didn't descend all too far from the Col du Granier (but I was grinning the whole time because it was fast, smooth, and beautiful). Rather than continuing south in the valley, we added in another pass, the Col de la Cluse. Less than 400 meters of climbing and hardly any traffic. 

![Ben riding up a mountain road. To the left are the meadows, forests, and mountains of the Chartreux range, with some clouds](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Ben-riding-up-a-mountain-road.-To-the-left-are-the-meadows,-forests,-and-mountains-of-the-Chartreux-range,-with-some-clouds.jpg)

Two passes done, but there was still a lot more to come! We turned off from the main road from the Col de la Cluse (which already is a minor road) onto a 1-lane road that led downward sharply. It was small and steep enough that I worried if this really was where we were supposed to go. It was. It leads into the Guiers vif gorge. We stopped near the bottom and marveled at the view of the exposed road on the other side of the gorge.

![The Chartreuse mountains and a tiny road with a wood railing running through it.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-The-Chartreuse-mountains-and-a-tiny-road-with-a-wood-railing-running-through-it-.jpg)

![A deep moutain gorge. On the right a narrow road with our two bikes parked next to it. On the left in the distance a road carved into a rock face.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-A-deep-moutain-gorge.-On-the-right-a-narrow-road-with-our-two-bikes-parked-next-to-it.-On-the-left-in-the-distance-a-road-carved-into-a-rock-face-.jpg)

![Ben in bike gear in front of a rock face from a quarry. He is very small compared to the mountain.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Ben-in-bike-gear-in-front-of-a-rock-face-from-a-quarry.-He-is-very-small-compared-to-the-mountain-.jpg)

The other side of the Guiers vif gorge was where we were headed. It's hard to capture the scale in pictures. The road is cut into the side of the mountain and on the right there is a drop of several hundred meters. 

![Ben riding on a "balcony road", with a deep gorge on the right and overhanging rocks with safety netting on the left.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Ben-riding-on-a-_balcony-road_,-with-a-deep-gorge-on-the-right-and-overhanging-rocks-with-safety-netting-on-the-left-.jpg)

Next we turned the other direction and went up the next spectacular gorge, the Gorges du Guiers Mort. Here the road stays close to the river. This is also the start of the long climb to the Col de Porte.

![Ben riding through a narrow gorge on a winding road, with tall rocks on both sides.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Ben-riding-through-a-narrow-gorge-on-a-winding-road,-with-tall-rocks-on-both-sides-.jpg)

The gorge was awesome, but we didn't enjoy the second half of the climb to the Col de Porte too much. We probably rode a bit too fast (Ben was trying to keep up with two Brits on road bikes and I was trying to keep up with Ben). By the time we got to the pass, I was feeling meh. BUT: Then we had an absolutely amazing descent into Grenoble that lifted my spirits again. Another day completed.

![Pass selfie on the Col de Porte. I'm making an unhappy face. There are a lot of stickers on the pass sign.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Pass-selfie-on-the-Col-de-Porte.-I'm-making-an-unhappy-face.-There-are-a-lot-of-stickers-on-the-pass-sign-.jpg)

Our hotel was in a suburb of Grenoble. Grenoble is the kind of city where even slightly run down suburbs look awesome because there's always the backdrop of the surrounding mountains. 

![Railroad tracks, industrial buildings, and a backdrop of tall mountains in the evening light.](/images/2026-alps/20260911-Railroad-tracks,-industrial-buildings,-and-a-backdrop-of-tall-mountains-in-the-evening-light-.jpg)

The next day we had the rare experience of starting the day with a flat stretch. 

![A mural of a woman sitting on a chair in a field, with one dog on her lap, one at her feet. To the left a bike is lying on the ground.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-A-mural-of-a-woman-sitting-on-a-chair-in-a-field,-with-one-dog-on-her-lap,-one-at-her-feet.-To-the-left-a-bike-is-lying-on-the-ground-.jpg)

We followed the Romanche river to the base of the Col de la Morte. 

![Ben biking on the shoulder of a wide highway in a deep mountain valley. The sun is still low](/images/2026-alps/20260912-Ben-biking-on-the-shoulder-of-a-wide-highway-in-a-deep-mountain-valley.-The-sun-is-still-low.jpg)

That is a long ascent with just over 1000 m of climbing. It didn't feel that bad though, as we had company from a local who had just started a crazy 300-km-and-who-knows-how-many-passes day ride that he anticipated finishing sometime at 3 am. He just rode and chatted with us with well over half an hour until we reached the pass.

![Top of the Col de la Morte. There's a big and mostly empy parking lot to the right.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-Top-of-the-Col-de-la-Morte.-There's-a-big-and-mostly-empy-parking-lot-to-the-right-.jpg)


After descending the Col de la Morte, there was some up and down until we reached the base of the Col d'Ornon. 

![Ben on his bike pointing at a sign for the Col d'Ornon](/images/2026-alps/20260912-Ben-on-his-bike-pointing-at-a-sign-for-the-Col-d'Ornon.jpg)

Now we were on a pass that was part of the #TourDeFrance route this year, as evidenced by the road paintings. 

![A large Slovenian flag and "POGI" painted on the road up the Col d'Ornon. Ben is ahead on the road on his bike.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-A-large-Slovenian-flag-and-_POGI_-painted-on-the-road-up-the-Col-d'Ornon.-Ben-is-ahead-on-the-road-on-his-bike-.jpg)

The pass itself was a little boring, but with pretty scenery and very little traffic.

![The Col d'Ornon pass sign. A disembodied hand is holding up a bag of Tour de France branded potato chips. Ben is standing in the background on a meadow. My bike is leaning against the sign.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-The-Col-d'Ornon-pass-sign.-A-disembodied-hand-is-holding-up-a-bag-of-Tour-de-France-branded-potato-chips.-Ben-is-standing-in-the-background-on-a-meadow.-My-bike-is-leaning-against-the-sign-.jpg)

On top of the Col d'Ornon we had to make a decision: Take the direct descent to Le Bourg-d'Oisans. Or descend only partway and then add another whole pass, the Col de Solude, which promised gravel sections, tunnels, and more. We were both willing to do the later, and I'm so glad we did. The Col de Solude was a highlight. 

![A switchback on a narrow road.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-A-switchback-on-a-narrow-road-.jpg)

A practically car-free ascent on many switchbacks and an absolutely spectacular descent on a "balcony road" with great views!

![In the foreground houses with corrugated steel roofs, some of them with a lot of rust. In the distance tall mountains.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-In-the-foreground-houses-with-corrugated-steel-roofs,-some-of-them-with-a-lot-of-rust.-In-the-distance-tall-mountains-.jpg)

![The portal to a narrow tunnel cut into the side of the mountain. The road is one lane and there appears to be a drop-off on the left.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-The-portal-to-a-narrow-tunnel-cut-into-the-side-of-the-mountain.-The-road-is-one-lane-and-there-appears-to-be-a-drop-off-on-the-left-.jpg)

![A narrow road on the side of the mountain, with a deep valley to its right. On the other side of the valley is a tall mountain with a similarly exposed road on it.](/images/2026-alps/20260912-A-narrow-road-on-the-side-of-the-mountain,-with-a-deep-valley-to-its-right.-On-the-other-side-of-the-valley-is-a-tall-mountain-with-a-similarly-exposed-road-on-it-.jpg)

When making our decision to add the Col de Solude, one consideration was that the next day would have us do the famous L'Alpe d'Huez climb. Ben and I agreed that if our legs and bodies didn't feel right, we'd be okay skipping that or only doing two thirds before turning off to the Pas de la Confession. To nobody's surprise: We ditched all those considerations the next day and rode all the way up the Alpe. How could we not? 

![A sign near the bottom of the Alpe d'Huez climb, noting that there are 13 km to go and the next kilometer has an average gradient of 11.1%](/images/2026-alps/20260913-A-sign-near-the-bottom-of-the-Alpe-d'Huez-climb,-noting-that-there-are-13-km-to-go-and-the-next-kilometer-has-an-average-gradient-of-11.1.jpg)

![Looking down from the Alpe d'Huez climb over some of its switchbacks and the opposing side of the valley.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-Looking-down-from-the-Alpe-d'Huez-climb-over-some-of-its-switchbacks-and-the-opposing-side-of-the-valley-.jpg)

![A sign on top of the Alpe d'Huez climb, showing a stylized person on a bike and the text "Arrivée." Ben is on his bike, looking at his handlebars. A hotel called Le Chaix in the background.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-A-sign-on-top-of-the-Alpe-d'Huez-climb,-showing-a-stylized-person-on-a-bike-and-the-text-_Arrivée._-Ben-is-on-his-bike,-looking-at-his-handlebars.-A-hotel-called-Le-Chaix-in-the-background-.jpg)

From L'Alpe d'Huez we took the Pas de la Confession, which isn't much of a pass but another beautiful balcony road. 

![Ben is standing over his bike and looking down into the Romanche valley.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-Ben-is-standing-over-his-bike-and-looking-down-into-the-Romanche-valley-.jpg)

Next up: The Col de Glandon/Col de la Croix de Fer (they share the approach from this side). We did not enjoy the climb: Long steep straight sections through the woods. And once the terrain gets more interesting, there are some drops followed by even steeper ramps. Only the last third or so was enjoyable. This also marked the highest point of our trip.

![Ben is riding on an otherwise empty road just below the Col de la Croix de Fer.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-Ben-is-riding-on-an-otherwise-empty-road-just-below-the-Col-de-la-Croix-de-Fer-.jpg)

![The earthen dam of the Lac de Grand Maison. A service track is zig-zagging up the dam.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-The-earthen-dam-of-the-Lac-de-Grand-Maison.-A-service-track-is-zig-zagging-up-the-dam-.jpg)

![The upper end of the Lac de Grand Maison. To the left and in the distance the road toward the Col de Glandon. The water level on the lake is quite low.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-The-upper-end-of-the-Lac-de-Grand-Maison.-To-the-left-and-in-the-distance-the-road-toward-the-Col-de-Glandon.-The-water-level-on-the-lake-is-quite-low-.jpg)



From the Croix de Fer we took the direct descent to Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne, home of my favorite knife brand, Opinel. I had planned a variation of getting there that would have taken us on the [Route de l'Opinel](https://www.opinel.com/fr-fr/le-musee/la-route-de-lopinel), a tourist route about Opinel history. But since that would've involved more climbing we skipped it. At least our route took us through the Opinel roundabout. We spent the night in a hotel near the (very new) train station. 

![A roundabout with a sculpture of an Opinel knife in the center.](/images/2026-alps/20260913-A-roundabout-with-a-sculpture-of-an-Opinel-knife-in-the-center-.jpg)
