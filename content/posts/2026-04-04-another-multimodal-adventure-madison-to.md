Over the past year, there have been a lot of news that indicate that that may be changing, sooner than anticipated. But I'm not going to get my hopes up. A grad school friend was going to be in Chicago. My significant other had a hotel room booked already and so I might as well join! The plan: Leave early on Saturday, catch a mid-day train and arrive in Chicago in the afternoon. Have some fun, eat a lot, sleep. Do the reverse the next day.</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj4U_01BK2wIhi36okGDhpsRR_7y2rm9O5EgQ-O5Tki1_XG_geexdCLZ49sxwbJbxRSb2ODuIIUIrSJIHGsgHqbARCUGNXGM_MjJBJyMRR3eGixWI_9N-B-ZFafGm6FtkMm0XeSSOdZpNs2EPpWABxg5GA0dZzar1B9358LZ5yqi6IHlq3CwvUj_uJnHAg/s3158/PXL_20260328_121426035.RAW-01.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="My bike riding shadow in the morning sun" border="0" data-original-height="2368" data-original-width="3158" height="480" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj4U_01BK2wIhi36okGDhpsRR_7y2rm9O5EgQ-O5Tki1_XG_geexdCLZ49sxwbJbxRSb2ODuIIUIrSJIHGsgHqbARCUGNXGM_MjJBJyMRR3eGixWI_9N-B-ZFafGm6FtkMm0XeSSOdZpNs2EPpWABxg5GA0dZzar1B9358LZ5yqi6IHlq3CwvUj_uJnHAg/w640-h480/PXL_20260328_121426035.RAW-01.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><p>This being March in Wisconsin, the weather is an important variable. March can have days warm enough for shorts and t-shirt. Or a blizzard. Or everything in between. On that spectrum, the weather forecast looked okay-ish: Cold but sunny on Saturday morning, with a predicted low of&nbsp;-7 C/20 F and a high of +7 C/45 F; and much warmer and mostly sunny on Sunday. Good enough for me.</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjdS-CuQp15RyVJCbzFr0I1CoWbAfLMgvj1sGgW2FxucYs63tiHFCU_zWvhRbKCIFDCBX2XkmuipUFQ0_IRstQfhXUPD4msaQlSJ0f3EmJhAmoID2xj13Dp_-_gpoKhcsVHGtIyGZiymT4-KYDmlaXrivnioqwbViAJ2gn8IlmI2b9x_ERtai1jo00tEQc/s4000/PXL_20260328_165848780.RAW-01.MP.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="A bike path just outside of Harvard. This was to reduce the amount of time I'd be riding while the sun wasn't up yet or very low. I departed Madison at 6:19 am, half an hour before sunrise. And yes, it was cold! A few degrees warmer than the forecast, but still well below freezing. I wore thick wool socks, neoprene shoe covers,&nbsp;and wool tights on my lower body. A&nbsp;wool baselayer, long sleeve wool jersey, and a softshell jacket on top. A buff to keep the wind out, plus a hat. I knew that my hands would be the weakest link and added a pair of chemical warmers to my gloves. Juust warm enough.&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;</p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjcrBe-1uoDEj49P0i9NfsluKN8L1RQ3mhGGILYmStTl_100f3yBy39bYTNICx8f354qMn0PdQBDgyFEL82810dnzdzT3kUKohtnOONYu_w6fsB4ESttLdKcIcJKlatsgm_QF4CjB91fwcGZk3jhObwbjY4fpEhnu0n8EeSWSK8eefkzvHZCfMAl3pQap0/s3409/PXL_20260328_115204605.RAW-01.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="Sunrise over Upper Mud Lake" border="0" data-original-height="3409" data-original-width="2557" height="400" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEjcrBe-1uoDEj49P0i9NfsluKN8L1RQ3mhGGILYmStTl_100f3yBy39bYTNICx8f354qMn0PdQBDgyFEL82810dnzdzT3kUKohtnOONYu_w6fsB4ESttLdKcIcJKlatsgm_QF4CjB91fwcGZk3jhObwbjY4fpEhnu0n8EeSWSK8eefkzvHZCfMAl3pQap0/w300-h400/PXL_20260328_115204605.RAW-01.jpg" width="300" /></a></div><br /><p></p><p>Aside from the cold, it was a beautiful morning. A clear sky, birds singing, not much wind, the sun just about to rise over the Madison lakes. I managed my effort to balance staying warm enough while not riding too hard. After all, it was 135 kilometers (80 miles) to Harvard, which this early into the season is a considerable distance. Some of the ill effects of the cold were that my contents of my water bottles turned into slush (but not wholly freezing at least!). And the wax on my chain was so hard that it caused my drive train to be noisy and imprecise.&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;</p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj-kAqFJBNx_qSZbxMScy8rQSuwxrphjnK_4wK_SppDK_IkQUmeu56wcNVcHRf-toUtNmmlpwNqkYrvZQ_4vVFx8yO0VY34PhCZdfn3mmSpN4lVARUAsqt823xCQSj3pQ-J9LMuAERX37vT_YoSUUirR7CrNEq8umHdDd-HMUped7qLW8MvS4cF0JcXq6c/s4000/PXL_20260329_180556817.RAW-01.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="A farm implement on an otherwise large fallow farm field." border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="3000" height="400" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj-kAqFJBNx_qSZbxMScy8rQSuwxrphjnK_4wK_SppDK_IkQUmeu56wcNVcHRf-toUtNmmlpwNqkYrvZQ_4vVFx8yO0VY34PhCZdfn3mmSpN4lVARUAsqt823xCQSj3pQ-J9LMuAERX37vT_YoSUUirR7CrNEq8umHdDd-HMUped7qLW8MvS4cF0JcXq6c/w300-h400/PXL_20260329_180556817.RAW-01.jpg" width="300" /></a></div><br /><p></p><p>The route I had created prioritized short distance and low traffic roads. It would be tight, but maybe I could increase my effort and cut a few kilometers off the planned route. However, as the sun rose not only did the temperatures go up but so did the wind. And it was not in my favor. So I had to resign to the fact that I would arrive much too early for the 1:35 pm train but had no realistic chance of catching the 11:35 am. Ah well. I dialed my effort back down and arrived in Harvard at 12:08, after 5h 48 minutes. My moving time was only 13 minutes less than that<span class="kY2IgmnCmOGjharHErah" style="-webkit-line-clamp: 3;"><span>—</span></span>when it's this cold, you really need to minimize the time stopped!&nbsp;Harvard has a very nice coffee shop, <a href="https://www.facebook.com/themugcoffeehouse/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">The Mug</a>, one block from the train station, and I slowly warmed myself up with a "golden oat latte."</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiuvLpEGojWYQUlvdLwFDSZ-5XJiGxYjTGsM1wwwzqwtor1xmAQFUeua43DWMC30tNQ9J3e34IwwhVo9vxyexCmdoPoKFTp3O7ANQrqgQbbRh_VLDrfb65wO2B8U02ycxk4BoTsYkem31uaoBzuWkUgodRSTfX0MLy2VWuT5jiri8b8roQB2dWXf8iwUQg/s4000/PXL_20260328_182349925.RAW-01.MP.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="My pink bicycle in the bike area of a Metra train" border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="3000" height="400" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiuvLpEGojWYQUlvdLwFDSZ-5XJiGxYjTGsM1wwwzqwtor1xmAQFUeua43DWMC30tNQ9J3e34IwwhVo9vxyexCmdoPoKFTp3O7ANQrqgQbbRh_VLDrfb65wO2B8U02ycxk4BoTsYkem31uaoBzuWkUgodRSTfX0MLy2VWuT5jiri8b8roQB2dWXf8iwUQg/w300-h400/PXL_20260328_182349925.RAW-01.MP.jpg" width="300" /></a></div><p></p><p>The Metra train is great for bringing on your bike: Most cars have a bike rack and no extra ticket is required for the bike. With some trepidation I watched a flag across the street: Clearly it was quite windy, and the wind appeared to come from the west. My route back was going northwest overall. I was mentally prepared for this and resisted the temptation to take a closer look at the wind forecast. It's not like I could do anything about the wind. I biked to the train station, and after less than 19 hours in Chicago I was on the train again. I got back on the bike in Harvard at 12:16 pm.&nbsp;</p><p></p><div style="text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhLXkCs-6Fz4RgHw0hilkF2DEg99h6ticK2ofERUo09lbaVyecCQEZzXC7AyF__8F0Lw-Sq1dvm1fTA9pcsAMqUPDjmpDJbPoRVuiSegXLgL7VAxhEZEvaLnC01j7XVfQaYLkB3Vk0MxMBoZjSQLN6mahPberQAgEgmPIkREfnX_MNgRy_bUnPABrRb0BI/s4000/PXL_20260329_175344639.RAW-01.MP.jpg" imageanchor="1"><img alt="A bird of prey circling" border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="3000" height="400" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhLXkCs-6Fz4RgHw0hilkF2DEg99h6ticK2ofERUo09lbaVyecCQEZzXC7AyF__8F0Lw-Sq1dvm1fTA9pcsAMqUPDjmpDJbPoRVuiSegXLgL7VAxhEZEvaLnC01j7XVfQaYLkB3Vk0MxMBoZjSQLN6mahPberQAgEgmPIkREfnX_MNgRy_bUnPABrRb0BI/w300-h400/PXL_20260329_175344639.RAW-01.MP.jpg" width="300" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><p>As I had anticipated, it was windy! The wind seemed to be from the south rather than the west, though. Better than expected. My route for the return trip was different. Head west to Beloit, northwest to Orfordville, and then straight north back into Madison. The first segment felt hard. Boring landscape, tired legs, strong crosswinds. I was resigned to the day being a long slog. But hey, the fact that it was much warmer and I had no pressure to catch a train counterbalanced this. And while the crosswind made me feel very slow, I noticed that my actual average speed was pretty decent. And whenever there was a segment of road heading north, the tailwind felt amazing. My mind increasingly focused on the fact that for the last 30 km of my ride the wind would be in my back <i>the whole way</i>! You forget your tired legs and butt. It is suddenly quiet, except for the hum of your tires spinning at 22 miles an hour. The distance remaining on your bike computer ticks down rapidly.
