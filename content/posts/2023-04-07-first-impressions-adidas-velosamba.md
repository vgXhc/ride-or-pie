<p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/PXL_20230401_223620269~2.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4080" data-original-width="3072" height="640" src="/images/PXL_20230401_223620269~2.jpg" width="482" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><p>For years, my casual cycling shoe of choice was the <a href="https://amzn.to/3Kkaerg">Giro Rumble VR</a>. I extensively reviewed <a href="http://ride-or-pie.blogspot.com/2017/05/giro-rumble-vr-review-one-year-out.html">the shoes on this blog</a>. While I've had my complaints about them, on balance they were a great shoe and the ended up lasting longer than I thought. I actually still have an unused spare pair in the closet. But when it was time to retire the current pair, I was curious to try something different.&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC07730.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="426" src="/images/DSC07730.jpg" width="640" /></a></div>&nbsp;<p></p><p>The Adidas Velosamba struck me as an interesting option. I'm German, and even though I never owned an actual Samba shoe, the aesthetic appeals to me. Add to that they're vegan and Adidas was selling them at a discount.&nbsp;</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC07733.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3442" data-original-width="5163" height="426" src="/images/DSC07733.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br /><br />I bought the shoes just before winter and so for several months I didn't actually have a chance to wear them. But now winter is nearing its end and I've worn them enough to write up my initial impressions. These impressions are heavily influenced by my experience with the Giro Rumble VR: Are the Velosambas better? Is that even a sensible question to ask? We'll return to that at the end of the post.&nbsp;<p></p><p><br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC07737.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3033" data-original-width="4550" height="426" src="/images/DSC07737.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><p></p><p>Let's start with the looks. The shoes come in 7 different styles, from the classic black with gum sole and white stripes, some more colorful options, and finally an all white shoe. If you know me, you would be surprised that I went for the all-white shoe! Is a white cycling shoe actually practical? I had my doubts, but in the end those were the ones on sale at the steepest discount while still being available in my size. So I was willing to take a gamble. When the shoes arrived, I was really pleased: The white is bright, and slight iridescent under light. The upper material is mostly very smooth plastic and probably cleans up fairly well. At the front of the shoe, there is some fake suede leather, which probably is more challenging to keep clean. The laces are white as well, with black ends as a color accent.&nbsp;</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC07738.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3074" data-original-width="4611" height="426" src="/images/DSC07738.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><p></p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>Functionally, the Velosamba has a few nice features: The lace retention system is well thought out. You can tuck the laces under an elastic band, which is easily lifted with a little pull tab. The cutout for the SPD cleats also seems to be well thought out: These so far have been the least crunchy bike shoes I've ever owned! Maybe this will change as the shoes wear, but it's unlike the Giros, which were crunchy from the get go.&nbsp;</p><p>&nbsp;<table align="center" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" class="tr-caption-container" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><tbody><tr><td style="text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC07739.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="426" src="/images/DSC07739.jpg" width="640" /></a></td></tr><tr><td class="tr-caption" style="text-align: center;">Clever lace retention tab<br /></td></tr></tbody></table></p><p>&nbsp;</p><p>Speaking of the sole: it seems to be a decent compromise between stiffness for biking and flex for walking. This makes them a great choice for my use case, where I want want a shoe that can easily transition from the bike to walking around town. It appears that the Velosamba is cut lower around the ankles and the heel compared to the Giros. This probably provides less support but probably won't be an issue for me: I 
often ride with shoe laces barely tightened, but I can see how for some 
people this might be a problem on longer rides/walks. At any rate, I have neither biked nor walked for longer distances in the shoes so far, and so I'll hold off on a final verdict. </p><p><table align="center" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" class="tr-caption-container" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><tbody><tr><td style="text-align: center;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4846" data-original-width="3231" height="640" src="/images/DSC07735.jpg" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;" width="426" /></td></tr><tr><td class="tr-caption" style="text-align: center;">Nicely recessed cleats make for a quiet walk<br /></td></tr></tbody></table></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC07735.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;">&nbsp;</a></div><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">&nbsp;</div>My main concern so far is the breathability of the shoes. Adidas proudly (annoyingly?) pronounces on the insole ,"End Plastic Waste" and states that the shoes is partially made from recycled plastic. Greenwashing aside, the material does indeed appear plastic-y and breathable at all. Again, I haven't worn the shoes enough to truly judge this.<p></p><p>So let's come back to the comparison with the Giro Rumble VR. In a head to head comparison, 

Adidas wins in these areas:&nbsp;</p><ul style="text-align: left;"><li>looks&nbsp;</li><li>less crunch</li><li>lace retention&nbsp;</li><li>walking/standing comfort (to be confirmed)<br /></li></ul><p>The Giros are ahead in:&nbsp;</p><ul style="text-align: left;"><li>breathabilty</li><li>price</li><li>foot support</li></ul><p>Once I've had the chance to wear the Velosambas for a few months I'll post a follow-up to these first impression. So far I'm really happy with my purchase.</p><p><a href="https://amzn.to/3KKizpK">Adidas Velosamba at Amazon&nbsp;</a></p><p><a href="https://www.adidas.com/us/the-velosamba-vegan-cycling-shoes/HQ6708.html">Velosamba at Adidas</a> <br /></p>
