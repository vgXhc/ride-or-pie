<br />
Jacques and Allison's hospitality continued the next morning: after waking up we were greeted with a chai latte, yogurt with berries and other breakfast goodies.
<br />
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">
<a href="/images/IMG_0765.JPG" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="320" src="/images/IMG_0765.JPG" width="240" /></a></div>
Breakfast was great, our legs weren't. Yesterday's 95 km and the hills had taken their toll and it was obvious that today would have to be a short day. Our camping guide showed a promising looking campground in Frelighsburg, about 45km from our current location. Jacques told us that <a href="http://www.village.frelighsburg.qc.ca/pages_anglais/home.html">Frelighsburg</a> was a very nice little town, and so we said goodbye to our hosts and headed off into the cloudy day.<br />
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">
<a href="/images/IMG_0767.JPG" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="240" src="/images/IMG_0767.JPG" width="320" /></a></div>
The first 15 kilometers along the Missisquoi River were fortunately flat, but we could still hear our legs complaining.<br />
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">
<a href="/images/IMG_0766.JPG" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="240" src="/images/IMG_0766.JPG" width="320" /></a></div>

On our club's 300 km brevets, Route Scenic is the one big climb. At that point, you've already ridden 160 km and so I wasn't quite sure how bad that hill really was. Well, it was a tough climb because it was long, but it was less steep than what we had encountered on previous days. We made a quick stop to check out the <a href="http://www.vindeglace.com/chapel.htm">Chapelle Sainte-Agnès</a>, a pseudo-romanesque chapel built by <a href="http://www.westmountexaminer.com/News/Local/2011-02-14/article-2239016/The-end-of-an-era-at-Henrietta-Antony-Inc./1">a rich Montreal antique dealer</a> to complement her <a href="http://www.vindeglace.com/vineyard.htm">vineyard</a>. <br />

<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">

The descent into Abercorn was fun, as usual, even though I slowed down a bit more than usual because of the wet roads. In Abercorn we encountered this old schoolhouse that had been completely <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarn_bombing">yarn bombed</a> -- "knit graffiti welcome"<br />

<a href="/images/IMG_0779.JPG" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="400" src="/images/IMG_0779.JPG" width="300" /></a></div>
Other than that, there wasn't anything in Abercorn and we continued west, now again in unknown territory. Of course, there was more climbing, making the biketopus severely unhappy. Bravely, she ascended the first couple of climbs, but at the bottom of YAFH (yet another f****** hill) we had to stop and only after a force-fed Clif Bar and lots of encouragement could we continue<br />
.<br />
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">
<a href="/images/IMG_0780.JPG" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="400" src="/images/IMG_0780.JPG" width="300" /></a></div>
Fortunately, the steep and hard climbs gradually gave way to more manageable rollers. The whole time we were never more than a few kilometers away from the US border, probably contributing to the emptiness of the landscape.<br />
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">

Just before reaching Frelighsburg, we encountered the best downhill so far: dead straight, no intersections, cars, or driveways, and smooth pavement. I bombed down there at a top speed of 75 km/h, and even the usually timid biketopus enjoyed herself at her personal <a href="http://bikesnobnyc.blogspot.ca/2011/06/indignity-of-commuting-by-bicycle-soul.html">woo-hoo speed</a>.<br />

The campground was about 5km outside of town, only a few hundred meters from the border crossing into Vermont. <a href="http://campingecologique.com/infos_en.html">Camping Écologique</a> was the best campground of the trip: our site was under large trees, right next to a gurgling creek -- pretty much the only sound to be heard there. A bonus feature were the two cute kittens that were hanging out in the common area. One of them followed us all the way to the campsite before jumping on the ATV of the a staff member to be taken back.<br />

After setting up our tent, I quickly rode back into town to buy groceries for the evening. On the way I spotted this guy on the shoulder of the road:<br />
<div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">
<a href="/images/IMG_0797.JPG" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="300" src="/images/IMG_0797.JPG" width="400" /></a></div>
It wasn't quite obvious in which direction he was headed and so I didn't carry him across the road. Fortunately, by the time I came back he was no longer there.<br />
<br />
This wasn't the last wildlife of the day: While we were sitting next to the firepit, dusk slowly surrounding us, I suddenly spotted a skunk! This visitor wasn't exactly welcome and our knowledge of what to do in case of skunk encounter was spotty. I made hissing noises towards it but that scared the biketopus more than the skunk... Fortunately, the skunk seemed to be more interested in insects than us and continued on its way. Quickly we consulted <a href="https://www.google.com/search?hl=en&amp;q=What+to+do+skunk+camping">teh Google</a> for skunk advice and were relieved to read that them spraying humans is actually not that common.<br />
<br />
Nonetheless, after dinner and spotting the skunk in the distance a couple more time we made sure to hang up all our food and other smelly items. As we didn't have anything to hang up the trash, I decided to walk to the trash containers near the campground exit. At this point it was pretty dark and misty. I was happy to have arrived at the container without any further wildlife encounters -- or so I thought. Just as I was about to throw the trash bag into the container, my headlamp reflected back from the eyes of three raccoons who were sitting right in the container, giving me a "What do <i>you</i> want here? This is <i>our</i> trash" look. Well, I decided to just hang my bag on the fence around the containers and quickly retreated to our tent.<br />
