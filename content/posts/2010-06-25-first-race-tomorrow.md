My identity as a cyclist is definitely not that of a racer. Yeah, I like going fast and pushing myself, but<br />
<ul><li> I'm not competitive;</li>
<li> I dislike the idea of depending on other people's riding (and potentially crashing);</li>
<li>I dislike the kind of macho crap that is very prevalent in the <a href="http://www.bikeforums.net/forumdisplay.php?41-Road-Cycling">road cycling section</a> of <a href="http://bikeforums.net/">bikeforums.net</a>;</li>
<li>I don't want to drive to a ride.</li>
</ul>But nonetheless I have registered for the <a href="http://sites.google.com/site/corningraceteam/corning-curcuit-race">Corning Circuit Race</a> tomorrow. Why? Well, for a couple of reasons. First, the race will take place on the <a href="http://www.theglen.com/">Watkins Glen International Speedway</a>, a Nascar track. Aside from it being a closed course and therefore presumably being safer for racing, I have a longstanding connection to Nascar racing. I <i>love</i> car games like <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Need-Speed-Shift-Playstation-3/dp/B001TORSHO?ie=UTF8&amp;tag=riorpi-20&amp;link_code=btl&amp;camp=213689&amp;creative=392969" target="_blank">Need for Speed</a>, <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Colin-McRae-Rally-Mac/dp/B000EIP1B6?ie=UTF8&amp;tag=riorpi-20&amp;link_code=btl&amp;camp=213689&amp;creative=392969" target="_blank">Colin McRae Rally</a>, the <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_%28series%29">Lotus series</a> -- and I played the 1994 <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASCAR_Racing">NASCAR Racing</a> extensively. I distinctly remember not liking the Watkins Glen track very much, as it was the most difficult one, with all those crazy turns. When I moved to Ithaca the first time and saw that the town of Watkins Glen was very close to it, it took me a while to realize that it was <i>that</i> Watkins Glen. After all, the race tracks in the video games are very much disembodied places for a German teenager who had never been to the US. Long story short: I really like the idea of racing the Speedway. The second reason for racing is that Watkins Glen is in biking distance. I can take the bus to Enfield, and from there it's only 35 km (unfortunately including a bunch of climbing) to the Glen. The race starts at 4:30, giving me a comfortable window to get there and back. The way back after the race might be painful, but whatevs.<br />
<a name="goals">I have four goals for the race:</a><br />
<ol><li>no DNF due to mechanical problems</li>
<li>no crash</li>
<li>no crash</li>
<li>not finishing last</li>
</ol>As preparation for the race there is a bunch of things:<br />
<ul><li><a href="http://www.gpsies.com/map.do?fileId=rpclbszojsyjnowj">map</a> <a href="http://www.gpsies.com/map.do?fileId=pgnrpilnefwtltta">out</a> <a href="http://www.gpsies.com/map.do?fileId=ymjuxyzriiwreubm">the</a> ride and write a cuesheet (I left my GPS in a friend's car in California)</li>
<li>adjust rear derailleur</li>
<li>switch seat posts, so that the racing saddle is at the correct height</li>
<li>maybe buy a <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Headsweats-Spin-Cycle-Cap-Black/dp/B0025UGU3O?ie=UTF8&amp;tag=riorpi-20&amp;link_code=btl&amp;camp=213689&amp;creative=392969" target="_blank">cycling cap</a> to prevent annoying sweat-in-eye problems</li>
<li>go for a short ride to shake out the legs</li>
<li>study&nbsp;<a href="http://www.trackpedia.com/wiki/Turn_by_turn_guide_to_the_Watkins_Glen_track">turn-by-turn guide</a> of the track (just kidding)</li>
<li>maybe shave legs, in case I fail to achieve goals 2 and 3...</li>
</ul>Keep your thumbs pressed (as the Germans say) -- race report will be posted soon. <br />
