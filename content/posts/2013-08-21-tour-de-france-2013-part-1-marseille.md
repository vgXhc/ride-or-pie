As part of our summer work-vacation trip with the <a href="http://biketopus.blogspot.ca/">biketopus</a>, I had a chance to see the 100th edition of the <a href="http://www.letour.fr/">Tour de France</a>. We managed to tweak our schedule so that I was able to see four stages, one in Marseille and three in and around Gap in the French High Alps. Lots of pictures were taken, and here is the first installment of photo essays.<br />
<br />
Marseille was the finish of the <a href="http://www.letour.fr/le-tour/2013/us/stage-5.html">fifth stage</a> of the Tour. After three stages on Corsica and the team time trial in Nice, this stage was a mostly flat 228km from Cagnes-sur-Mer to the Marseille beaches.<br />
<br />
Having never been to the Tour in person, I didn't have a good idea of what to expect -- how many people are going to be there? How close to the action can you get? What's the best place to be at? -- and therefore just made up things on the go. I knew that on such a flat stage there most likely would be a mass sprint at the finish and so I decided to head out to the finish area. Wanting to get a good spot, I headed out there on my <a href="http://www.levelo-mpm.fr/">Le Velo bike share bike</a> several before the projected arrival.<br />
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536156391/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" src="https://farm6.staticflickr.com/5515/9536156391_7ffefc38eb_z.jpg" /></a>

<br />
The roads were already closed to car traffic, and as I assumed they'd be closed for cyclists, too, I dropped off my bike and continued on foot along the Avenue du Prado.<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536158165/" style="clear: left; float: left; margin-bottom: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" title="Two kilometers to go by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Two kilometers to go" src="https://farm4.staticflickr.com/3761/9536158165_64d0076974.jpg" /></a>
<br />
While walking along the wide street and past the 2 km mark a bunch of cyclists passed me on the closed-off section of the road -- I now regretted having dropped off my bike instead of also riding on the same road that the pro riders would soon be on.<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536160421/" style="clear: left; float: left; margin-bottom: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="274" src="https://farm3.staticflickr.com/2818/9536160421_c35f8be065.jpg" width="500" /></a>
<br />
Soon after, the first team buses appeared.<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536162509/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="335" src="https://farm4.staticflickr.com/3770/9536162509_439bdb80f6.jpg" width="500" /></a> <br />
It was quite funny to watch...<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538952662/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="375" src="https://farm6.staticflickr.com/5514/9538952662_f7ff9b1461.jpg" width="500" />&nbsp;</a><br />
... how carefully they navigated through "la flamme rouge", the marker for the final kilometer. On the very first stage of the Tour in Corsica, a team bus had <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VuQW_strQFM">crashed into the finish line</a>, causing all kinds of complications.<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536170193/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="331" src="https://farm8.staticflickr.com/7370/9536170193_8398bf363a.jpg" width="500" /></a>
When I arrived at the final straightaway it was still pretty empty. I walked as far as I could, but access was restricted at the final 200 meter marker. I walked back a little bit and secured myself a nice front row spot at about 220 meters from the finish. Now the waiting began, as the riders wouldn't be arriving for another two hours.
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538969194/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="375" src="https://farm6.staticflickr.com/5481/9538969194_24737a998a.jpg" width="500" /></a>

Fortunately, the Tour organizers try hard to keep spectators entertained, and that's what "<a href="http://www.lacaravanepublicitaire.com/">La Caravane Publicitaire</a>" is for. It's basically a big parade of all the main sponsors, giving away free crap.
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538971404/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="375" src="https://farm8.staticflickr.com/7333/9538971404_b45f8d1d08.jpg" width="500" /></a>
The quality of the floats ranged from "well done and funny"...
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536185771/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="500" src="https://farm3.staticflickr.com/2849/9536185771_ddcdab0532.jpg" width="500" /></a>
...to "rather strange."
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538964966/" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="368" src="https://farm8.staticflickr.com/7400/9538964966_4b05d1b944.jpg" width="500" /></a>
What was at least as much fun as watching the caravane itself, was watching how excited people would get over a free bottle of water, polyester hats, or a pack of candy.
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536178997/" title="Raining Haribos! by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Raining Haribos!" height="375" src="https://farm8.staticflickr.com/7350/9536178997_4aa6898034.jpg" width="500" /></a>
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538960050/" title="Kids' race arriving by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Kids' race arriving" height="382" src="https://farm8.staticflickr.com/7308/9538960050_96dbbaa165.jpg" width="500" /></a>
After the caravane had come through, two groups of young riders came racing through. First the really young ones...<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9536175003/" style="clear: left; float: left; margin-bottom: 1em; margin-right: 1em;" title="Untitled by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Untitled" height="375" src="https://farm3.staticflickr.com/2808/9536175003_78c6eb56f1.jpg" width="500" /></a>
... then the somewhat older ones sprinting it out.
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538976010/" title="Here they come! by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Here they come!" height="375" src="https://farm4.staticflickr.com/3770/9538976010_6d6731e26b.jpg" width="500" /></a>
The long waiting had taken its toll and I felt increasingly roasted under the Mediterranean sun, but finally the riders arrived! As expected, it was a mass sprint and as such it was over very quickly.
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538978130/" title="200 meters to go by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="200 meters to go" height="247" src="https://farm6.staticflickr.com/5475/9538978130_7b8fe211e7.jpg" width="500" /></a>
Going all out on the last 200 meters. At this point, Gert Steegmans was in front, but he was only doing the work for his team mate Mark Cavendish right behind him, who would eventually <a href="http://www.letour.fr/le-tour/2013/us/stage-5/classifications.html">win this stage</a>. If you zoom in on the picture you can see the big crash unfolding in the background.<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538982238/" title="Crash! by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Crash!" height="375" src="https://farm4.staticflickr.com/3789/9538982238_55847361d8.jpg" width="500" /></a>
The mass crash looked pretty bad at first, but it seems that nobody got hurt too badly -- which is kind of surprising, given how fast the riders must have been.
<br />
<a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/haarald/9538984342/" title="Ouch by Hobbes vs Boyle, on Flickr"><img alt="Ouch" height="500" src="https://farm6.staticflickr.com/5489/9538984342_2b2b000038.jpg" width="500" /></a>
Scrapes and bruises, however, were plentiful. Ouch!<br />
