<p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00938.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3342" data-original-width="5013" height="426" src="/images/DSC00938.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><p>I bought a <a href="https://crustbikes.com/collections/frames/products/lightning-bolt-canti">Crust Lightning Bolt frame (the cantilever brake version)</a> and here are notes and photos from the build. <br /></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">Frame and fork weight <br /></h3><p>It's always helpful to weigh the frame and fork before building a bike. My XL/61cm frame weighs 2.22 kg (4.9 lbs), the fork comes in at 0.89 kg (1.96 lbs).</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00792.jpg" style="clear: left; float: left; margin-bottom: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="213" src="/images/DSC00792.jpg" width="320" /></a></div><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00787.jpg" style="clear: right; float: right; margin-bottom: 1em; margin-left: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3738" data-original-width="5607" height="213" src="/images/DSC00787.jpg" width="320" />&nbsp;</a></div><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;">&nbsp;</div><br /><h3 style="text-align: left;">Color</h3><p></p><p>When the production version of the Lightning Bolt came out I was very close to buying it right away, but the brown color really turned me off. On the box, it's called "Champagne Brown," which sounds ridiculous but actually makes some sense: Yes, it's brown, but a brown with a light sparkle to it. When I sent a photo of the bike to my mom, she responded, "Oh, a golden bike! How pretty!" I've come to the conclusion that the color is actually pretty nice and classy.<br /></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">Headset </h3><p>I went with a cheap-ish <a href="https://amzn.to/33N3AVI">Tange-Seiki Levin</a> headset for the build. Roller bearing headsets are hard to come by these days, and my low-trail Gunnar works fine with a regular headset. Reading other people's build reports, one thing that was mentioned that headset installation was difficult because of an oversized crown race seat. My own measurements showed that the diameter is almost 0.1 mm above spec. I tried getting the race on with my crappy DIY tool made from a PVC pipe, but it quickly became clear this wouldn't work. I took it to the LBS and they got it on no problem -- actually the mechanic complimented the "tone" of my fork when hammering it in. Uh huh. Pressing the cups also took a lot of effort and I was glad to have borrowed a friend's <a href="https://amzn.to/3ppfv5P">Park Tool headset press</a> rather than relying on a DIY solution.</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00901.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="426" src="/images/DSC00901.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">Shifters</h3><p>I haven't bought a new frame for over 10 years, and so I figured I may build it up with the nice parts from my stash. I had a NOS set of Dura Ace 7800 downtube shifters that I had been saving for years. This is the relatively rare 10-speed version that can be switched between friction and indexed shifting in the rear.</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00909.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3283" data-original-width="4925" height="426" src="/images/DSC00909.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br /><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00911.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3784" data-original-width="5676" height="426" src="/images/DSC00911.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><h3 style="text-align: left;">Fenders</h3><p>Transferring over the Velo Orange fenders from my SOMA was unproblematic. The distance from the fender to the seat/chainstay bridges appears to be slightly smaller than on the SOMA, meaning I didn't need as many washers.&nbsp;</p><h3 style="text-align: left;">Cranks and Bottom Bracket<br /></h3><p>The chainstays on the Lightning Bolt are seriously shaped and crushed to maximize tire and crank clearance. I suspect I may be able to get use a shorter bottom bracket than on the SOMA and get a lower Q factor that way. The cranks are <a href="https://www.interlocracing.com/shop/product/ird-crankset-defiant-compact-double-46-30t-2801?search=crank">IRD Defiant</a> subcompacts with 46/30 rings.<br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00800.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3611" data-original-width="5416" height="426" src="/images/DSC00800.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br /><p>And indeed: With a 113 mm bottom bracket (IRD actually recommend a 118 mm), there is a lot of clearance:</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00923-2.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="1365" data-original-width="2048" height="426" src="/images/DSC00923-2.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br />&nbsp;<p></p><p></p><p></p>A 110 mm bottom bracket would definitely work, and maybe a 107 mm would 
fit as well -- the limiting factor may be the small chainring clearance 
and possible derailleur issues.<p></p><p>Because of the shaped chain stays, I opted for shellacked twine as a chainstay protector. I ran out of twine and so it's a little short for now.<br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00939.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="426" src="/images/DSC00939.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br /><p>The prototype for this frame had a frame-mounted rear cable hanger. The production version does not, and so I used a <a href="https://surlybikes.com/parts/cable_hanger">Surly hanger</a>. Space between the bolt and seat post is tight, and I filed down the hanger to make it work.</p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00921.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3123" data-original-width="4685" height="426" src="/images/DSC00921.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><p></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Derailleurs</h2><p>For the rear I transferred over an old Shimano STX RC, a mid-range 7-speed derailleur from the 1990s. In the front I installed a new Shimano CX-70 derailleur. I have this one on my Gunnar as well, as it was recommended as working well with sub-compact drivetrains. My own experience has been mixed -- there is a lot of trimming required to avoid chain rub. This seems to be the case on the Lightning Bolt as well, and I'll have to try fine tuning the positioning of the derailleur. It doesn't help that the big chainring has a small amount of wobble...</p><p>&nbsp;<table align="center" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" class="tr-caption-container" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><tbody><tr><td style="text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC01000.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="426" src="/images/DSC01000.jpg" width="640" /></a></td></tr><tr><td class="tr-caption" style="text-align: center;">Shimano CX-70 derailleur and IRD 46/30 crankset<br /></td></tr></tbody></table><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Seatpost</h2><p>Brooks saddles often require seatposts with a large amount of setback. On the SOMA I had a very hammock-like Brooks B17 Imperial mounted on a <a href="https://velo-orange.com/collections/seatposts/products/vo-grand-cru-seat-post-long-setback">Velo Orange Grand Cru long setback post</a>. On the first ride my position felt very wrong, and this was mostly due to the seat being too far back. I have since replaced the VO seatpost with a <a href="https://amzn.to/3fStii7">Thomson Elite</a>.</p><table align="center" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" class="tr-caption-container" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><tbody><tr><td style="text-align: center;"><a href="/images/IMG_20210530_132742987.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><img border="0" data-original-height="2325" data-original-width="3488" height="426" src="/images/IMG_20210530_132742987.jpg" width="640" /></a></td></tr><tr><td class="tr-caption" style="text-align: center;">Thomson Elite setback post with an old WTB saddle that has titanium rails (yay) but isn't all that comfortable (boo)<br /></td></tr></tbody></table><br /><p><br /></p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Decaleur</h2><p>The decaleur is the weakest link in the build at the moment. It's the cheap Velo Orange decaleur, which has worked surprisingly well on the SOMA. On this build, however, it ends up too low for my bag. Because the Lightning Bolt has less headtube extension than the SOMA, I can't get it up high enough with a bag as tall as mine. No decision yet on how to address this.</p><p><table align="center" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" class="tr-caption-container" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><tbody><tr><td style="text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC01002.jpg" imageanchor="1" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><img border="0" data-original-height="4000" data-original-width="6000" height="426" src="/images/DSC01002.jpg" width="640" /></a></td></tr><tr><td class="tr-caption" style="text-align: center;">Velo Orange decaleur: Too low for my tall bag<br /></td></tr></tbody></table><br />&nbsp;</p><h2 style="text-align: left;">Brakes</h2><p>Tektro CR-720s are my go to 
affordable cantilever brakes. I may eventually replace them with 
something more blingy, but they look nice and do their job.</p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00999.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3529" data-original-width="5294" src="/images/DSC00999.jpg" width="320" /></a></div><br /><h3 style="text-align: left;">Overall impressions</h3><p>Transferring parts over from one bike to another made for a quick and fun build. The only obvious flaw of the Lightning Bolt is a lack of cable guides for my generator hub. Eventually I'll clean up the cable routing, but with a frame at this price point and aimed at long-distance riders, guides on the fork and frame would have been nice. I don't anticipate making any substantial changes to the build in the foreseeable future, except for the decaleur. Stay tuned for a separate post on ride impressions. <br /><br /></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="/images/DSC00929.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" data-original-height="3627" data-original-width="5440" height="426" src="/images/DSC00929.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br /><br />
