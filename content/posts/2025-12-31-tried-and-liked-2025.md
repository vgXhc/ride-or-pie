My 
accidental Serotta Colorado frame purchase has turned out to be a good 
investment. I finally found a nice fork for it (a scarily light Columbus
 Minimal), and I did a bunch of rides on the bike this year. As long as 
the pavement is reasonably smooth, I'm really enjoying the ride, despite
 the 25/28 mm tires.&nbsp;</div><div>- Assos Arm Protectors:&nbsp;<a data-saferedirecturl="https://www.google.com/url?hl=en&amp;q=https://www.assos.com/us/arm-protector-188424.html&amp;source=gmail&amp;ust=1767286962896000&amp;usg=AOvVaw3-teT64WzQoJeBi5yI0iSx" href="https://www.assos.com/us/arm-protector-188424.html" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">https://www.assos.com/us/arm-protector-188424.html</a>
 Nice sun protection that works for my long arms and doesn't slide down 
match. And for an Assos product, it's remarkably reasonable in price</div><div>-
<a href="https://dairyroubaix.weebly.com/"> Dairy Roubaix</a>: This is an early-season gravel ride in the Wisconsin 
Driftless region. I used to do it regularly but hadn't done it since the
 pandemic. I had an absolute blast this year. The weather was great, as 
were the roads. I'll likely be back next year.</div><div>- A rear rack 
on my Stevens MTB. I added a rear rack to the early 2000s Stevens MTB 
that I have at my parents' place in Germany. Having the option to add 
panniers, in addition the Carradice saddle bag, is really nice. 
Should've done that a long time ago.&nbsp;</div><div>- Having as many front 
lights as bikes. My regularly used bikes all have dyno lights. And now I
 finally have at least one decent battery front light for all of the 
other bikes. No more annoying switching around of lights.</div><div>- Making bike videos: I'm still not fast or super skilled, but I enjoy filming, editing, and narrating my bike adventures. Here's my YouTube channel: <a href="https://www.youtube.com/@ride-or-pie/">https://www.youtube.com/@ride-or-pie/&nbsp;&nbsp;</a></div><div>&nbsp;</div><div><br /></div><h2 style="text-align: left;">Undecided</h2><div>-
 For my winter indoor riding I switched back from Training Peaks Virtual
 to Zwift. Zwift has a whole lot of annoying user interface issue that 
are much more glaring after coming back from TPV. I also liked the 
racing experience on TPV more. On the other hand, Zwift's gamification 
gets me to ride a lot, and it's important to be on the same platform as 
your real-life friends.</div><div>- TPU tubes. I still go back and forth
 between liking and disliking them. Just yesterday I had a flat that 
most likely wouldn't have happened with a butyl tube. While on other 
bikes the tubes have performed great.</div><div>- <a href="https://amzn.to/45oM6ip">Lumintop B01 front light:</a> One of the battery lights I acquired this year. It doesn't conform with the German cut-off regulations, but it does have a shaped beam that doesn't blind oncoming riders. And it's relatively cheap, has a replaceable battery and seems sturdy. I think I like it, but it hasn't been long enough to be sure.</div><div><br /></div><h2 style="text-align: left;">Disliked</h2><div>-
 Not having big goals. I struggled with motivation to ride in the first 
half of the year. I didn't have any events or tours planned for later in
 the season, and it showed. I'll have to figure something out for 2026.&nbsp;</div><p>&nbsp;</p>
