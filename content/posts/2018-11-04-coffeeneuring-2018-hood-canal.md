First Boston, and then last weekend I had the opportunity to visit  Seattle for the first time. Even though November is he rainiest time of  the year there, I </span></span><span class="passivevoice"><span data-offset-key="3562d-1-0"><span data-text="true">was determined</span></span></span><span data-offset-key="3562d-2-0"><span data-text="true"> to get a long bike ride in. Bike rental options in Seattle were much more reasonable than what I had found in Boston. Instead of paying over $100 for a fancy road bike, I was able to find a budget gravel bike <a href="http://pedalanywhere.com/">delivered to my hotel for less than $60</a>. My <a href="https://www.strava.com/routes/16029852">plan</a> was to retrace part of the route of the <a href="https://janheine.wordpress.com/2018/09/10/great-times-at-the-bq-un-meeting-2018/">Bicycle Quarterly Un-Meeting</a>. </span></span></div>

<span data-offset-key="ca19s-0-0"><span data-text="true">My bike was delivered by bike trailer at 9am. From Seattle, I took the ferry to Bremerton. The sky stood in stark division. South, there was sun and clear skies; north there were dark clouds and heavy rain. The ferry cut right through the middle, at times pounded on by the rain, at time sailing under the beautiful sun. By the time I disembarked in Bremerton, the weather was beautiful. It was clear enough to even catch a glimpse of the tall peaks of the Olympic mountains in the distance. </span></span></div>

<span data-offset-key="dtb4t-0-0"><span data-text="true">I navigated by cue sheet, missing a turn a mere 10 minutes into the ride, but once I was out of the city, navigation was easy After a good hour, I reached Belfair, at the far end of the Hood Canal. </span></span><span class="adverb"><span data-offset-key="dtb4t-1-0"><span data-text="true">Suddenly</span></span></span><span data-offset-key="dtb4t-2-0"><span data-text="true"> there was a lot of traffic. On a two-lane road with narrow or no shoulders at all that was irritating. Since I was out of water anyway, I used the opportunity for a brief stop at Belfair State Park. A strong wind blew over the water from the west. What was bothersome for me as a westbound cyclist was great for the kite surfers at the park. </span></span></div>

<span class="hardreadability"><span data-offset-key="9icvr-5-0"><span data-text="true">My only complaints now were the continuing stiff headwind and the </span></span></span><span class="adverb"><span data-offset-key="9icvr-6-0"><span data-text="true">equally</span></span></span><span class="hardreadability"><span data-offset-key="9icvr-7-0"><span data-text="true"> stiff sidewalls of my tires</span></span></span><span data-offset-key="9icvr-8-0"><span data-text="true">. My rental bike had <a href="https://amzn.to/2qumfSw">28-millimeter-tires of the stiffest variety</a>. And so the vibrations from the rough road surface </span></span><span class="passivevoice"><span data-offset-key="9icvr-9-0"><span data-text="true">were transmitted</span></span></span><span data-offset-key="9icvr-10-0"><span data-text="true"> straight into my body. In Wisconsin we have roads with potholes and buckles from winter's freeze-and-thaw cycles. But we do not have these long stretches of coarse chip seal surface that made me yearn for <a href="https://amzn.to/2JDorQh">wide, supple tires</a>. </span></span></div>

<span data-offset-key="f11u1-0-0"><span data-text="true">After reaching the Tahuya River, my route took me inland. From looking at the elevation profile, I knew that I was up for a tough climb. And indeed, Belfair–Tahuya Road delivered. The climb has an average grade over 10%, and I was happy that at least it was short. Once on top, the reward was a view of the Olympic mountains through tall trees. The road turned into a fun roller coaster, albeit a roller coaster with very rough tracks.&nbsp;</span></span></div>
