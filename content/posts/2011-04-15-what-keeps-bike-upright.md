It's rare that bicycles feature in the <a href="http://www.news.cornell.edu/"><i>Cornell Chronicle</i></a>, but today there is an article about interesting research by Cornell prof <a href="http://ruina.tam.cornell.edu/">Andy Ruina</a> on <a href="http://www.news.cornell.edu/stories/April11/bicycle.html">what prevents bikes from falling over</a>. The main finding is that conventional theories of self-stability -- the gyroscopic effect of the spinning wheels and trail of the front wheel -- are  not the main factors in keeping a bike stable. Ruina and his collaborators have constructed a bike (well, it doesn't look exactly like a bike) that has no gyroscopic effect and no trail -- but still stays upright on its own. Fascinating stuff with potential practical implications for bicycle design. <br />
<br />
There's also a <a href="http://bicycle.tudelft.nl/stablebicycle/stablebicycle640.mov">video</a> explaining the research, and here's a the <a href="http://www.scienceblogs.de/geograffitico/StableBicyclev34Revised.pdf">pre-print of the article</a> which will be published in <i><a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/">Science</a>.</i>
