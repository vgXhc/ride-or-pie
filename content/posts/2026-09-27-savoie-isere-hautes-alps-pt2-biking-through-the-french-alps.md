Here's part two of my photo essay about our trip through the French Alps


I had a plan for the next day: The Col de la Madeleine would be the pass for the day. But we'd approach it through the back door, via the switchbacks of the Lacets de Vernier, then the Col du Chaussy, and take a gravel road to near the top of the Madeleine. Well, that didn't work out: There was road work on the Lacets de Vernier. 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260914-Morning-view-over-the-area-near-the-train-Station-at-Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne.-The-sun-is-still-behind-the-mountains-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260914-Partially-obstructed-view-of-the-Montvernier-switchbacks,-with-a-village-in-the-foreground-.jpg)

So we took the main approach to the Madeleine. I ended up chatting with a nice Swiss cyclists for the last half hour of the climb.

![](/images/2026-alps/20260914-Two-loaded-cyclists-on-the-climb-to-the-Col-de-la-Madeleine.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260914-Selfie-in-front-of-the-monument-on-top-of-the-Col-de-la-Madeleine.-I'm-smiling,-kinda-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260914-The-road-on-the-northern-side-of-the-Col-de-la-Madeleine,-in-a-high-alpine-landscape.-Weathered-wooden-fence-posts-in-the-foreground-.jpg)

We spent the night in Albertville, in a huge hotel complex that we suspected was associated with the 1992 Winter Olympics. Next on the list: The Col des Cyclotouristes. Hard to skip a pass with such a name! From a connectivity perspective it's not very useful, which meant we had it practically to ourselves (no other cyclotouristes either). 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260915-View-back-toward-Albertville-(which-isn't-visible)-from-the-acent-to-the-Col-des-Cyclotouristes.-A-narrow-road,-meadows,-and-woods-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260915-A-smiling-selfie-in-front-of-the-Col-des-Cyclotouristes-(Alt-1330-m)-pass-sign-.jpg)

On the descent both Ben and I literally gasped when we turned around a corner and Mont Blanc appeared towering in the distance. 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260915-A-narrow-downhill-mountain-road.-In-the-distance,-Mont-Blanc-is-towering-over-the-surrounding-mountains.jpg)

To get to today's destination, Flumet, we needed to get over one more pass, the Col des Saisies. We didn't take the main road but ascended via the route toward the Signal de Bisanne. Hardly any traffic on that road but consistently steep and long. We both struggled. 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260915-Ben-riding-uphill-through-a-steep-switchback.jpg)

Saisies is a large ski station on top of the pass, which at this time of year was mostly deserted. No grocery store for us.

![](/images/2026-alps/20260915-A-tight-set-of-switchbacks-leading-to-a-bridge-with-flags.-Behind-the-bridge-a-mountain-village,-Flumet-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260915-View-out-of-the-window-of-a-room-onto-a-curvy-road-.jpg)

After a bad night of sleep, we tackle two passes today. The weather forecast predicted rain in the afternoon and so we get an early start. First, the Col des Aravis. The saddle I have on my gravel bike is named after this pass. It's a good one to ride. Only 500 m of climbing at reasonable grades, hardly any traffic, good views. 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-Ben-on-a-curved-mountain-road-in-a-shaded-valley.-In-the-distance-are-tall-mountains-lit-by-the-morning-sun-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-Ben-riding-on-a-curvy-mountain-road-with-rock-face-on-the-right-and-a-valley-on-the-left-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-View-from-above-of-a-curvy-mountain-road,-meadows-with-cows,-and-mountains-in-the-distance-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-Selfie-with-the-Col-des-Aravis-pass-sign,-which-is-made-from-concrete-and-has-a-large-split-in-in-it.-I'm-squinting,-sweaty,-and-look-unhappy-(but-am-not)-.jpg)

We're getting to the last pass of our trip. Another big name: The Col de la Colombière. We're doing it from the easy side, though. Which is a good thing as the clouds are getting closer and larger. 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-Ascent-to-the-Col-de-la-Colombière.-The-sky-is-mostly-cloudy.-On-the-edge-of-the-frame-is-Ben-in-his-red-bike-jersey-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-Towering-clouds-over-a-mountain-range.-In-the-foreground-a-village-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-The-top-of-the-Col-de-la-Colombière-pass.-A-pass-sign-full-of-stickers-in-the-bottom-left.-The-tops-of-the-mountains-are-close-to-the-clouds-.jpg)

The start of the pass has a lot of traffic and deserted ski resorts. Near the top it is very pretty though. Because I know this is the last pass of the day, I increase my effort and arrive on top out of breath. A fast and thrilling descent follows -- we beat the rain!

![](/images/2026-alps/20260916-Église-Saint-Jean-Baptiste,-a-small-church-in-Le-Reposoir,-with-a-backdrop-of-mountains.jpg)

We're at the hotel, we're done with the mountains. Only one more flat stage tomorrow. A lot of tension releases in my body. I lie on the bed, completely relaxed, and take a nap. Aaaah. 

![](/images/2026-alps/20260917-Morning-in-a-mountain-valley.-A-meadow-in-the-foreground.-There-is-some-mist-shrouding-the-mountains-in-the-distance.-The-sun-is-sitting-low-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260917-Ben-on-a-narrow-road.-Some-green-houses-in-the-near-distance-and-mountains-in-the-far-distance-.jpg)

![](/images/2026-alps/20260917-Selfie-of-Ben-and-me-in-front-of-Geneva's-water-fountain-.jpg)

The next day we ride back to Geneva and part ways. Ben flies back from there and I take the train to my parents' place in Germany. What a trip.
